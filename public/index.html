02 PLUG AND PLAY

Trained an AI model on a proprietary dataset of 800+ records

During my time at Plug and Play I ran first-pass review on 800+ pitch decks. I reviewed each one and kept the verdicts in a structured spreadsheet. This process was very repetitive, and as I looked at my growing dataset I thought: I can train an AI model on this data.

THE MODEL
83%Same invest-or-pass call as me, on meetings it never saw
85%Picks the deck I backed over one I passed (ROC AUC 0.85, against 0.75 for adding up the star ratings)
-40%Time I spent reviewing decks

It’s a gradient-boosted model, tested with grouped, nested cross-validation so no meeting leaks into its own test, and exported to ONNX so it runs inside the app.

NetZero

Makes your Python use less energy.

NetZero takes a Python repository, finds the functions that do the most work, and rewrites them to use less energy. It started at HackEurope in Stockholm, a trip a few friends and I flew out for together.

Each function gets tests first, then three rewrites. A rewrite is only kept if it passes the tests, gives the same answers as the original on recorded calls, and measurably cuts the energy of every call.

STACK
Python, FastAPI, Postgres, Next.js
LINKS

Jev Events

Turn any inbox, calendar or chat into typed, semantic events.

An open-source TypeScript library built on TypeSafe’s Jev. You ask a question in plain English, like “does this email need a reply today?”, and Jev answers it for every new email, invite or message your users connect. Your code runs on the answer.

The events are typed from your own questions, so a typo is a compile error, and every action starts in dry-run. It works with Gmail, Google Calendar, Slack and Twitch today, and you can try it on a live Twitch chat in your browser.

TypeScript, Jev, OAuth, Next.js

05 NYC

The move to NYC

I’m moving to New York City to go all in on startups. I just finished my Bachelor’s in Computer Science, and I have decided I want to continue my learning in a different way.

From the moment I learned what a startup even was, I haven’t been able to look away. There’s a switch in my brain I can’t turn off, that gets me excited when I find a new idea I can work on, or build something that someone mentions has been annoying them.

So why move to NYC? The startup scene in Belgium is growing, but it just doesn’t compare to what’s available here. People here work harder, move faster, and have a completely different mindset: one I resonate with much more. I wanted to learn from more experienced people here, so that’s what I’m doing.

I want to join a growing team with a LOT of ambition. Almost everything I know, I learned by building. So that’s what I want to keep doing: build a lot, build fast, and build big.
